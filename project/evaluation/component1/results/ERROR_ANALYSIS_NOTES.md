# Error analysis notes

Hand-written, not regenerated by `run_baselines`. Every pattern below carries a count computed from the 30 sampled test errors of that task
(`<task>_errors.csv`; sampled in a fixed hash order, not by difficulty; the "k of n" counts are over those 30 rows, not over all errors).
Anything without a count is labelled **hypothesis**. Nothing was tuned on these rows. The errors files were identical before and after the
one-row train correction (same 118 / 89 / 30 / 194 error counts).

## FR/NFR (best by validation: TF-IDF + linear SVM; 118 of 935 test rows wrong)
- Source: 27 of 30 sampled errors are FR_NFR rows, 3 are PROMISE. Direction: 16 are FR->NFR, 14 are NFR->FR.
- Form: median 14 words (range 7-28), 3 of 30 under 10 words, 0 of 30 over 40 words; 28 of 30 contain *shall* or *must*; 0 of 30 contain more than one sentence.
- FR->NFR (n=16): 7 of 16 contain a quality-attribute keyword from the rule baseline's cue list (so 9 of 16 contain none); 5 of 16 contain *registration*, *permission*, *user management*, *integrat-* or *understand-*; 6 of 16 contain *privacy*, *access*, *account*, *admin*, *customer*, *login*, *password*, *browser* or *compatib-*.
- NFR->FR (n=14): 3 of 14 contain a rule cue; 2 of 14 contain a number or time unit.
- PROMISE errors (3): 2 of 3 end with a stray quote character (a source artefact); 0 of 3 have more than one sentence; word counts 14, 20, 22.
- Hypothesis: many NFR->FR rows are label-boundary cases or annotator noise, because 11 of the 14 contain no quality keyword and read as plain functional statements. This cannot be shown from counts alone and needs a manual check.

## NFR subtype
### Security (best by validation: keyword rules; 89 of 1,285 test rows wrong)
- Direction: 22 of 30 sampled errors are false positives (true 0, predicted 1), 8 are false negatives. Sources: PURE 24, `security_requirements` 5, PROMISE 1.
- PURE false positives: 17 of 24 PURE errors; 17 of the 17 contain a security cue word from the rule's list in the normalized text the rule reads (16 of 17 in the original text shown in the errors file) (the most frequent are *password*, *secure*, *auditable*, *attacks*, *security*). Their lengths range from 7 to 56 words; 2 of the 17 are over 40 words.
- PURE false negatives: 7 of 24 PURE errors; 0 of 7 contain a cue word, so the rule cannot find them. Lengths are 8, 11, 14, 23, 30, 44, 51 words (3 of 7 under 15 words, 2 of 7 over 40).
- Overall: 6 of 30 sampled errors are over 40 words; 9 of 30 contain *shall* or *must*.
- Hypothesis: the false positives are regulatory or manual sentences that mention security words without being security requirements under the PURE labelling; the counts show cue words are present, not why the label is 0.
- Note: this rule is "best" by validation, but PURE validation is one document (HIPAA), so the selection is noisy.

### PROMISE multi-label (best by validation: keyword rules; 43 test rows)
- 30 of 30 sampled errors are PROMISE rows; 15 of 30 have two or more true labels.
- 16 of 30 predict **no label at all**. By true label: LF 8, O 2, MN|O 2, A|PE 1, SE 1, LF|US 1, MN 1. Among the 12 sampled errors whose true label contains LF, 9 predicted nothing.
- 9 of 30 predict a non-empty strict subset of the true labels (for example A|PE predicted as PE, 2 rows); 5 of 30 predict at least one label that is not true.
- 30 of 30 texts end with a stray quote character (a source artefact); 8 of 30 have more than one sentence.
- Hypothesis: the empty predictions come from rows with no cue word for look-and-feel, operational or maintainability; for the rule model this follows from how the rule works (no cue, no label), so it says little about the data.
- Per-class test support is tiny (SC 1, FT 2, L 3, PO 0), so per-class numbers are not reliable.

## Ambiguity (best by validation: TF-IDF + logistic regression; the QuRE weak-word rule is reported in the same tables)
- Direction: 21 of 30 sampled errors are `ok` predicted as `defect`, 9 are `defect` predicted as `ok`. Sources: DS2 12, QuRE 8, DS3 5, DS4 3, DS1 2.
- ok->defect (n=21): 15 of 21 contain *should*, *must*, *may*, *can* or *might*; 3 of 21 contain *will*; 3 of 21 are over 40 words.
- defect->ok (n=9): word counts are 10, 10, 19, 20, 27, 32, 47, 57, 60, so 2 of 9 are short (10 words) and 7 of 9 are 19 words or longer; 3 of 9 are over 40 words; 6 of 9 contain a modal (*should*, *must*, *may*, *can*, *might*).
- 8 of 30 sampled errors are DS2 sentences starting "Systems supporting OM ...". Across the whole ambiguity task file there are 57 such rows (all splits), 30 labelled `ok` and 27 `defect`: near-identical template sentences carry both labels.
- Hypothesis: the defect->ok errors are long sentences whose single smell term is rare in training data. The counts show they are long, not that they hold a single rare term (not checked).
- Hypothesis: the template sentences make part of the error rate label noise by construction.
